AWORKFLOW / 03VIDEO REVIEW

I turned myself into a pig with AI [Free & Local VFX Tutorial]

The demonstration of MiniMax-H3's ability to handle multiple conditioning inputs (original video performance, character reference sheets, audio, and timed prompt cues) in a single pass to perform high-fidelity character replacement.

WHY IT MATTERS

This workflow functions as a high-end neural rendering pass. In a USD/Houdini pipeline, it would sit post-animation/lighting as a 2D replacement tool or be used in early pre-viz for realistic performance evaluation without full 3D rigging. It bridges the gap between 2D plate editing and 3D character integration.

  • ComfyUI
  • MiniMax-H3
  • KREA 2
  • SAM (Segment Anything Model)
  • H3-Upscaler
Inside the workflow · 4 takeaways
  • Timed Emotion Cues: Embedding timestamps directly into text prompts to synchronize AI-generated expressions with the source video performance.
  • Character Sheet Conditioning: Using a grid of character views/emotions as a reference image to maintain identity across video frames.
  • Inpainting for Plate Preservation: Restricting the AI model to a specific mask to keep the original background and body movements intact while swapping only the head.
  • SAM-to-Video-Pipeline: Integrating Segment Anything Model to automatically generate masks for complex objects (like head-mounted camera rigs) for removal.

AWORKFLOW / 04VIDEO REVIEW

3-Step Workflow To Master AI Realism (Full Tutorial)

Higgsfield SeeDream 5.0 Pro and Seedance 2.5 release, focusing on cinematic consistency through 'blocking maps' and location-anchored prompting.

WHY IT MATTERS

Currently a proprietary, web-hosted ecosystem. While it doesn't ingest USD directly, the method of using schematic blocking maps to drive prompt-spatial awareness is highly applicable to any multi-character generative workflow.

  • Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4
  • Soul Cinema
  • SeeDream 5.0 Pro
  • Seedance 2.5
  • Claude
Inside the workflow · 4 takeaways
  • Creation of 3-panel character sheets (close-up, front, back) to lock actor appearance
  • Top-down blocking diagrams mapped to prompts to control spatial character placement
  • Use of specific background 'anchors' (skylights, lamps) to maintain continuity across reverse angles
  • Employing 'character-back wipes' to hide temporal discontinuities between AI video cuts
